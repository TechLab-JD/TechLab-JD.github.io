Where the
Good Dog Waits

At the end of every life, a dog is waiting.

Fifty-two books · being written now

When a life ends, the first thing you meet isn't a bright light or a tunnel. It's a dog — ancient, calm, unhurried — sitting as though he has been waiting for you a very long time and has made his peace with it.

He is Death's companion, the chosen escort for the last walk. The room you wake in dissolves into a hallway built from your own memory, and the two of you walk it together: past windows you can only watch, toward doors you choose to open — while he asks the quiet questions that let you finally set down what you were still carrying.

Fifty-two souls. Fifty-two hallways. One good dog who has walked them all. In the spirit of Departures, Coco and Mushishi — gentle, unhurried, and quietly certain that no one should make the last walk alone.

From Book One

There was a dog near the door. Lying down, watching her with a stillness she recognized immediately — not the stillness of waiting, but the stillness of something that had been patient for a very long time and had made peace with it.

“So there really is something like you,” she said.

The dog lifted his head. “You’re not surprised.”

“I’ve sat with enough people at the end to know something else was always in the room with us.”

— The Porch Swing
