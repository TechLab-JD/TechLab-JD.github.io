// THE GRADE

Quality-of-Life Index, year by year

A composite score (0–100) blending income, growth, jobs, prices, housing affordability, wages, poverty and life expectancy — scored on an absolute scale against the best readings in modern U.S. history, not curved against other years. The grade is the headline; the three pillars below it are the truth — a single letter can't capture a country, so treat it as a conversation-starter, then watch the pillars pull apart. Full method below.

The three pillars behind the grade

Each pillar scored 0–100 on its own history; the overall grade is their average. The composite can look flat while the pillars pull hard against each other.

// THE DREAM

Do kids still out-earn their parents?

The most literal measure of the American Dream: the share of 30-year-olds who earn more than their parents did at the same age (inflation-adjusted), by the year they were born. It has roughly halved.

// COMPARE

Then vs. now

Pick any two years and see how life changed across every measure. A dash means the data isn't available for that year — either it wasn't collected that far back (most measures start between 1929 and 1984; only prices reach the 1910s), or it hasn't been published yet: annual series like income, poverty, life expectancy and emissions lag a year or more, so the comparison defaults to the latest year with broad coverage.

// DESPAIR

Deaths of despair & addiction

Some of "it feels worse" shows up in mortality, not money. Drug overdose deaths and suicides — what researchers call deaths of despair. Overdose deaths roughly doubled between 2015 and their 2022 peak, then fell in 2023-24; recorded suicides climbed to ~48,000 by 2018, dipped during the pandemic, then hit a record ~49,700 in 2022 (counts are final through 2019 and provisional for 2020-22). Shown for context, not folded into the grade.

// IMMIGRATION

Immigration & enforcement

Two flows, by year: people granted green cards (lawful permanent residence) and formal deportations (removals), with voluntary/administrative returns for context. Fiscal years, from DHS. Shown for context — not folded into the grade, since whether these are "good" or "bad" is a value judgment, not a data point.
